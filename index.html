Birth-Specific Prep
4-6 min

On the exercise ball:

Hip Circles – 1 min each direction
Sit on ball, feet flat and wide. Make big slow circles with your hips like you're stirring a pot. Go clockwise for 1 min, then counter-clockwise. Keep your upper body relatively still – movement comes from hips/pelvis.
Pelvic Tilts – 1-2 min
Sit tall on ball. Tilt pelvis forward (belly out, slight arch in back), then tilt back (tuck tailbone under, round lower back). Move slowly and smoothly. Think "cat-cow" but seated. This mobilizes the lower spine and pelvis.
Gentle Bounce or Figure-8s – 1-2 min
Light bouncing: small gentle bounces, keeping feet grounded. OR trace a figure-8 pattern with your hips (combine circles going in opposite directions). Both open the pelvis and encourage baby to descend.

Then off the ball:

Deep Squat Hold – 30-60 sec
Feet wider than hips, toes turned out. Lower down into a deep squat. If heels lift, put a rolled towel or yoga mat under them. Hold onto furniture or door frame if needed for balance. Let your belly hang between your thighs. Breathe. This opens the pelvis 20-30% more than standing.
Belly Breathing – 1-2 min
Sit or lie comfortably. Breathe in slowly through nose (4 counts), letting belly AND ribs expand outward. Exhale slowly through mouth (6 counts), letting everything soften. Practice relaxing your jaw, shoulders, and pelvic floor on the exhale. This is the breathing you'll use during labor.

Why: Improves pelvic mobility, fetal positioning tolerance, and your ability to relax while uncomfortable. That skill matters more than strength.

What This Prepares You For

  • Tolerating upright labor longer
  • Faster recovery between contractions
  • Easier position changes
  • Lower perceived exertion during active labor

What to Avoid

  • HIIT, "burn," or chasing soreness
  • Long workouts "while you still can"
  • New strength goals
  • Anything that spikes pelvic pain or exhaustion

Fatigue close to term increases discomfort without benefit.

The Rule

If you miss a day, do nothing to "make it up." Consistency beats volume. Finish feeling capable, not depleted.
